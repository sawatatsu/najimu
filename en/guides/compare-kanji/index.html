You may know what you want to call your baby while still being unsure how to write the name in kanji. Keep the reading fixed, try a few spellings, and look at each one beside the surname. neim helps you explore kanji for a reading and compare name ideas.

We use the fictional example 山田 たろう (Yamada Taro). You can try that same reading in the existing interactive demo. The website uses a prepared surname and reading. To enter your own, use the iPhone app. Its main input and comparison screens are in Japanese; this English guide does not mean the entire app is translated.

1. Choose the surname and the reading

In the app, enter the surname, its reading, and the reading you want for the given name. Writing the given name in hiragana makes it easier to hold the sound steady while comparing kanji. For example, keep たろう (Taro) as the reading to begin with.

The web demo displays 山田 たろう. Select a box in the given name to open kanji suggestions for that part. Web selections last only while the page is open; reloading returns the demo to its initial state.

2. Choose kanji by reading segment and meaning

Choose a kanji for part of the reading, such as た (ta) and ろう (ro). Read the suggested readings and meanings before selecting each character. Replacing one character, as in 太郎 and 太朗, can change the appearance and associations of the name.

If you do not find the combination you have in mind, try splitting or joining the reading. This changes the segment to which you assign a kanji. A suggested combination does not mean that everyone will interpret its reading in the same way.

3. Compare alternatives beside the surname

In the app, move left and right through kanji combinations for the same reading. Look at the surname and given name together: consider the number of characters, the density of their shapes, and how the full name sounds aloud. The web demo also has arrows for moving between prepared examples.

Changing the typeface or inverting the name and background lets you inspect the same characters in another presentation. These are display settings, not scores of a name’s quality or suitability.

4. Keep your ideas and read them again

The app automatically saves completed kanji combinations on your device and reflects later edits to saved ideas. Read the full name again and revisit any meaning or pronunciation you are unsure about. The sharing feature creates an image of the displayed name that you can send to a destination you choose.

The web demo does not save or sync ideas to the app. Make a separate note of any web example you like, and use the app to compare it with your own surname and reading.

Common questions

Is this fortune-telling or a popularity ranking?
neim is a tool for comparing readings and kanji. It does not provide name-based fortune-telling or predictions of good or bad luck. The order of suggestions is not a ranking of quality or popularity.

Does it include every possible reading and kanji?
Readings and meanings are reference information and are not exhaustive. Check them yourself before making a final naming decision. As explained in the existing terms of use, suggestions do not guarantee acceptance when registering a name.

What does it cost, and which language is the app in?
The current app is free, with no paid purchases or subscriptions. Its main input and comparison screens are in Japanese. An English website does not mean that the entire app is translated. Check device compatibility and availability on the App Store.
